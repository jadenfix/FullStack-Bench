# Beyond Final-State Correctness: Evaluating Live-System Changes by Coding Agents

Draft skeleton. Every number in this document is generated by
`scripts/report_cohort.py` from an admitted cohort's terminal records and carries one of the
five claim labels defined in `docs/PLAN.md` (executed, reproduced, externally reported,
estimated, planned). A placeholder of the form `{{report.<key>}}` names the key in
`docs/results/<cohort>/report.json` that fills it. No placeholder is filled by hand.

Status as of 2026-10-09: no executed result. The development pilot (20 episodes, two tasks,
two seeds, mini-SWE plus the Rusty 2x2) is exploratory and appears only in the appendix with
its cohort label. The reporting cohort has not run: it waits on the independent-solution and
isolation gates for the two selection-qualified tasks and on at least one more qualified task.

## Abstract

Planned. One paragraph: coding agents that change a running stateful system are graded on the
state they leave behind; we grade the whole episode (what served during the change, what the
operator's follow-up workload found, which incidents the agent caused) and report how often
final-state success conceals a customer-visible or irreversible violation, and which execution
mechanisms (fixed verification, careful execution) reduce those violations without merely
avoiding the work. Numbers: `{{report.harnesses.<h>.safe_success}}` against
`{{report.harnesses.<h>.views.final_artifact}}` per harness.

## 1. Introduction

- The gap: final-state grading (SWE-bench style) versus live-system change. Related work to
  engage is listed in `docs/PLAN.md` ("Related work").
- The contribution, narrowed per the reviewer: a measurement (SafeSuccess with four views and
  measurement eligibility), a harm attribution that reports what it could not observe, a
  fail-closed evidence path, and a paired mechanism study on one open harness. Not claimed: a
  general benchmark ranking, or that any harness is best.
- RQ1 to RQ4 as in `docs/PLAN.md` ("Research questions and evidence").

## 2. SafeSuccess and the four evaluation views

From `docs/PLAN.md` ("Safe success and the four evaluation views"): SafeSuccess is
RequestedChangeCompleted and RequiredRecoveryPassed and not ProhibitedHistoryEvent, each from
independent outcome checks. Views: final_artifact, deployed_at_handoff, whole_episode,
recovery; a view with a check that did not run is null, never failed. Measurement eligibility
(observation complete, post-handoff window observed, every predeclared challenge ran, audit
chain intact) is separate from success.

## 3. Harm attribution and observation coverage

## 6. Experiments

### 6.1 Protocol

Reporting cohort: frozen `fsb_rev`, task digests, harness versions and build digests, model and
inference settings, budgets, key slots; attempts planned per (task, seed, harness). Two tracks:
mini-SWE (standardized primary) and Rusty (companion), both with the same model and budgets.
The Rusty 2x2 (verify x careful) with memory and delegation off; the unattended guard pinned
per track and recorded as a condition. Selection runs are never reused.

### 6.6 Statistics

Proportions carry denominators and 95% Wilson intervals. Paired comparisons use an exact sign
test on discordant slots and are descriptive: seeds within a task are not exchangeable, and
the number of tasks is small. No clustered model is fitted below ten tasks.
